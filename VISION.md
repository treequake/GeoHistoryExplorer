# Vision

GeoHistory Explorer exists to expand the observable universe of historical Earth systems by making accumulated evidence explorable without sacrificing provenance, uncertainty, disagreement, or scientific integrity.

The engine organizes evidence; it does not determine truth.

## Scientific Purpose

Historical Earth-system research often depends on fragmentary, unevenly preserved, and disagreeing sources: chronicles, catalogs, field reports, archival descriptions, maps, instrumental re-analyses, translations, and later interpretations. GeoHistory Explorer should help researchers inspect those materials together while preserving the evidentiary texture that makes them scientifically useful.

The first domain is historical seismology. The architecture should still be broad enough for future historical Earth-system packs, such as volcanic events, floods, landslides, coastal change, climate observations, and other source-grounded phenomena.

## North Star

Every architectural decision should increase humanity's ability to observe, compare, and explore historical Earth systems while remaining faithful to the underlying evidence.

## Boundaries

GeoHistory Explorer is not primarily a GIS viewer, AI product, or general web app. It is durable scientific infrastructure organized around inspectable evidence packages.

The durable object is the **GeoHistory Pack**. Viewers, QGIS projects, AI summaries, notebooks, and future applications are downstream consumers of the pack.

## Scientific Integrity Rules

- Preserve original source information wherever legally and practically possible.
- Preserve provenance on every meaningful object and derived artifact.
- Preserve uncertainty and disagreement rather than collapsing them prematurely.
- Represent stated precision only; do not fabricate exact dates, coordinates, geometries, or confidence.
- Keep deterministic, inspectable transformations ahead of interpretation.
- Make each pipeline stage useful and reviewable on its own.

## Long-Term Test

Researchers should still be able to inspect a GeoHistory Pack in 2050, understand how it was built, trace every derived assertion to its source basis, and distinguish source claims from later interpretations.
